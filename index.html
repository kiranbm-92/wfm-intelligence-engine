Capacity Planning vs. Erlang C

Capacity Planning (this engine): a workload / productive-hours based FTE requirement — the monthly-to-6-month staffing number used for hiring and budget decisions.

For interval-level Service Level staffing (e.g. 80/20 in 30 seconds), Erlang C / interval staffing methodology is required — a separate calculation. Any occupancy shown on this page is a monthly capacity-utilization indicator, not an Erlang-C service-level result.

How Capacity Planning Works in Workforce Management

Step 1: Workload Calculation

Convert volume and AHT into total workload hours.

Step 2: Available Capacity

Convert paid hours into productive hours using shrinkage, then apply the planning buffer to size Required FTE.

Step 3: Gap Analysis

Compare required FTE vs current headcount.

Step 4: Hiring Plan

Apply attrition and ramp curves to project hiring needs.

Key Capacity Planning Terms

FTE (Full-Time Equivalent)
Number of agents required to handle workload.
Shrinkage
Time agents are paid but unavailable.
Occupancy
Percentage of available (productive) time agents spend actively handling work. A derived output, not a Required FTE input.
Attrition
Rate at which employees leave.
Ramp-Up
Time taken for new hires to reach full productivity.

Core Capacity Planning Formulas

Workload Hours = (Volume × AHT) / 3600
Productive Hours = Paid Hours × (1 - Shrinkage)
Required FTE = (Workload Hours / Productive Hours) × (1 + Buffer)
Occupancy = Workload Hours / (Effective HC × Productive Hours)
Net FTE Gap = Required FTE - Effective Headcount

Capacity Planning FAQs

How do you calculate required FTE in a call center?

Required FTE is calculated using workload, shrinkage, and buffer assumptions. Occupancy is a resulting output metric, not an input to Required FTE.

What is shrinkage in workforce planning?

Shrinkage is the percentage of paid time not spent handling work.

How does attrition impact hiring plans?

Higher attrition increases hiring requirements to maintain staffing levels.

What is a good occupancy rate?

Typically 75–85% depending on service levels; above 85% raises burnout and SLA risk.
